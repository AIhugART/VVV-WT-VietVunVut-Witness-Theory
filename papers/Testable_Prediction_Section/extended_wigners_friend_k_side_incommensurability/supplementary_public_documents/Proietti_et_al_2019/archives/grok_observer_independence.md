\## Abstract



The scientific method relies on facts established through repeated measurements and agreed upon universally, independently of who observed them. In quantum mechanics, the objectivity of observations is not so clear. This is most markedly exposed in Wigner’s thought experiment, where two observers can experience seemingly different realities.  



Through recent no-go theorems constructing an extended Wigner’s friend scenario with four observers, this question has become accessible to experimental test. In a state-of-the-art six-photon experiment, we realize this scenario and violate the associated Bell-type inequality by five standard deviations. Assuming locality and free choice, this implies that quantum theory must be interpreted in an \*\*observer-dependent\*\* way.



\---



\## Introduction



The observer’s role as the final arbiter of universal facts is challenged in quantum mechanics due to the \*\*measurement problem\*\*. Wigner’s friend thought experiment highlights the conflict:



\- A friend inside a lab measures a photon in superposition and records a definite outcome (fact from their perspective).

\- Wigner outside describes the entire lab as being in a superposition and can verify this via interference.



This raises the question: Can the facts established by different observers be reconciled into a single, \*\*observer-independent\*\* framework?



\### Extended Wigner’s Friend Scenario



The paper considers two inner observers (Alice’s friend and Bob’s friend) and two outer observers (Alice and Bob). Alice and Bob can choose to measure either:



\- \*\*A₀, B₀\*\*: The friend’s record (memory) → friend’s fact.

\- \*\*A₁, B₁\*\*: Joint measurement on system + memory → their own fact.



Under assumptions of \*\*Locality (L)\*\*, \*\*Free Choice (F)\*\*, and \*\*Observer-Independent Facts (O)\*\*, the correlations must satisfy a Bell-type inequality.



\---



\## Main Result



\*\*S\_exp = 2.416^{+0.072}\_{-0.075}\*\*



Violation of the inequality \*\*S ≤ 2\*\* by \*\*more than 5 standard deviations\*\*.



This demonstrates incompatibility between quantum mechanics and the three assumptions (L + F + O).

\## Experimental Setup



\- Three high-brightness, high-purity polarization-entangled photon-pair sources (S₀, S\_A, S\_B) at 1550 nm using Sagnac interferometers.

\- Temporal multiplexing to suppress multi-pair emissions.

\- Two type-I fusion gates to implement nondestructive measurements by the “friends”.

\- Alice and Bob perform either direct measurement of the memory photon (A₀/B₀) or a partial Bell-state measurement (A₁/B₁).

\## Supplementary Materials



\### 1. Towards a Loophole-Free Bell-Wigner Test



The experiment is subject to the same loopholes as standard Bell tests, but more challenging due to complexity.



\- \*\*Detection loophole\*\*: Requires high efficiency (η > 0.875 for loophole-free violation).

\- \*\*Locality \& Freedom of Choice\*\*: Requires space-like separation in an event-ready configuration.

\- \*\*New potential loophole\*\*: Whether A₀/B₀ truly measures only the friend’s memory.

\## Discussion



The violation implies that, if we accept locality and free choice, \*\*observer-independent facts cannot exist\*\*. Different observers can establish incompatible facts.



This supports interpretations such as:

\- Relational Quantum Mechanics

\- QBism

\- Many-Worlds (with privileged global view)



The definition of “observer” is purely physical (any system that extracts and stores information). Photonic memories qualify.
