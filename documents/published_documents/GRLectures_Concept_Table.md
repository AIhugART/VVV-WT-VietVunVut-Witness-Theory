# Compère & Fiorucci — Advanced Lectures on General Relativity (2017)
## Concept Extraction Table (LLM-Friendly)

> **Source**: Compère, G. & Fiorucci, A. (2017). *Advanced Lectures on General Relativity*. Lecture notes prepared for the Solvay Doctoral School on Quantum Field Theory, Strings and Gravity, Brussels, October 2017. arXiv:1801.07064v4. 4 chapters covering surface charges, 3D gravity, asymptotic symmetries, and rotating black holes.

| No. | Concept | Category | Definition |
|-----|---------|----------|------------|
| 1 | Killing Equation & Killing Vectors | Surface Charges & Conserved Quantities | A necessary and sufficient condition for a vector field $\xi^\mu$ to be an isometry of spacetime $(M,g)$: $\Lie_\xi g_{\mu\nu} = 0 \Longleftrightarrow \nabla_{(\mu} \xi_{\nu)} = 0$. In Minkowski spacetime, the 10 independent Killing vectors generate the Poincaré algebra (4 translations $a^\mu$ + 6 Lorentz transformations $b^{[\mu\nu]}$). Killing vectors are essential for defining conserved charges in gravity: to each isometry corresponds a conserved Noether charge $Q = \int_\Sigma \mathbf{J}$. |
| 2 | Conserved Stress Tensor from General Covariance | Surface Charges & Conserved Quantities | Any relativistic field theory in Minkowski spacetime admits a symmetric stress tensor $T^{\mu\nu}$ that is divergence-free on-shell: $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} \approx 0$. Defined via coupling to gravity: $T^{\mu\nu} \triangleq \frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}} \frac{\delta L}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}}$, then evaluated back on the flat metric. General covariance ($\delta_\xi \mathbf{L} = d(\cdots)$) directly implies $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} \approx 0$ without invoking Noether's theorem. From $T^{\mu\nu}$ one constructs a conserved 4-vector $J^\mu = T^{\mu\nu}\xi_\nu$ for each isometry $\xi^\mu$. |
| 3 | Presymplectic Potential ($\bm{\Theta}$) | Surface Charges & Conserved Quantities | The $(n-1,1)$-form arising from the boundary term when varying the Lagrangian: $\delta \mathbf{L} = \delta \Phi^i \frac{\delta \mathbf{L}}{\delta \Phi^i} - d\bm{\Theta}[\delta\Phi;\Phi]$. $\bm{\Theta} = \Theta^\mu (d^{n-1}x)_\mu$ collects the remnants of the inverse Leibniz rule applied to factorize $\delta\Phi^i$ without derivatives. It is ambiguous up to $\delta\mathbf{M}$ when adding $d\mathbf{M}$ to the Lagrangian, and up to $d\mathbf{B}$ from integration-by-part prescriptions. For Einstein gravity: $\Theta^\mu[h;g] = \frac{\sqrt{-g}}{16\pi G}(\nabla_\nu h^{\mu\nu} - \nabla^\mu h^\nu_{\ \nu})$. |
| 4 | Noether's First Theorem | Surface Charges & Conserved Quantities | A bijection between (i) equivalence classes of global continuous symmetries of the Lagrangian $L$, and (ii) equivalence classes of conserved Noether currents $J^\mu$. Two global symmetries are equivalent iff they differ by a gauge transformation or a trivially-zero on-shell symmetry. Two currents $J_1^\mu, J_2^\mu$ are equivalent iff they differ by $J_2^\mu = J_1^\mu + \partial_\nu k^{[\mu\nu]} + t^\mu$ with $t^\mu \approx 0$. For pure gauge theories (including GR), all Noether currents are trivial ($J^\mu \approx 0$), motivating the need for lower-degree conservation laws. |
| 5 | Generalized Noether Theorem (Barnich–Brandt–Henneaux) | Surface Charges & Conserved Quantities | For any theory with gauge symmetries: a bijection between (i) equivalence classes of gauge parameters $\lambda(x^\mu)$ that are field symmetries ($\delta_\lambda \Phi^i \approx 0$), and (ii) equivalence classes of $(n-2)$-forms $\mathbf{k}$ that are closed on-shell ($d\mathbf{k} \approx 0$). Two $\mathbf{k}$'s are equivalent if they differ on-shell by $d\mathbf{l}$ where $\mathbf{l}$ is a $(n-3)$-form. The conserved charge $Q = \int_{\partial\Sigma} \mathbf{k}$ is unambiguous despite the ambiguity in $\mathbf{k}$. This theorem provides the foundation for defining canonical surface charges in gauge theories. |
| 6 | Lower Degree Conservation Laws | Surface Charges & Conserved Quantities | In generally covariant theories, standard Noether currents are trivial ($J^\mu \approx 0$), but the corresponding charge $Q = \int_{\partial\Sigma} \mathbf{k}$ reduces to a surface integral of an $(n-2)$-form $\mathbf{k}$ on $\partial\Sigma$. This motivates seeking conserved $(n-2)$-forms $d\mathbf{k} \approx 0$ whose integrals on codimension-2 surfaces define conserved charges. Example: electric charge in electrodynamics from $\mathbf{k}_c = c F^{\mu\nu} (d^{n-2}x)_{\mu\nu}$ integrated on a 2-sphere. |
| 7 | Noether's Second Theorem | Surface Charges & Conserved Quantities | Each gauge symmetry of a Lagrangian gives rise to an identity among its equations of motion: $\frac{\delta\mathbf{L}}{\delta\Phi^i}\delta_\xi\Phi^i = d\mathbf{S}_\xi[\frac{\delta L}{\delta\Phi};\Phi]$, where $\mathbf{S}_\xi$ is an $(n-1)$-form proportional to the equations of motion and their derivatives. For Einstein gravity: $\mathbf{S}_\xi = -\frac{1}{8\pi G}(d^{n-1}x)_\mu \sqrt{-g}\, G^{\mu\nu}\xi_\nu$, vanishing on-shell by virtue of the contracted Bianchi identities $\nabla_\mu G^{\mu\nu}=0$. |
| 8 | Covariant Phase Space / Variational Bicomplex | Surface Charges & Conserved Quantities | A mathematical framework combining the spacetime manifold with the field (jet) space. Local coordinates are $(x^\mu, \Phi^i_{(\mu)})$ where $(\mu)$ denotes symmetrized multi-indices. Two Grassmann-odd differential operators are defined: the exterior derivative $d = dx^\mu \partial_\mu$ (horizontal) and the variational operator $\delta = \delta\Phi^i \frac{\partial}{\partial\Phi^i} + \cdots$ (vertical), which anti-commute $\{\delta,d\}=0$. A $(p,q)$-form has $p$ $dx^\mu$'s and $q$ $\delta\Phi^i_{(\mu)}$'s. The Lagrangian $\mathbf{L}$ is an $n$-form, the presymplectic potential $\bm{\Theta}$ is an $(n-1,1)$-form, and the presymplectic form $\bm{\omega} = \delta\bm{\Theta}$ is an $(n-1,2)$-form. |
| 9 | Presymplectic Form ($\bm{\omega}$) | Surface Charges & Conserved Quantities | The $(n-1,2)$-form defined as $\bm{\omega}[\delta\Phi,\delta\Phi;\Phi] = \delta\bm{\Theta}[\delta\Phi;\Phi]$. In Grassmann-even notation: $\bm{\omega}[\delta_1\Phi,\delta_2\Phi;\Phi] = \delta_1\bm{\Theta}[\delta_2\Phi;\Phi] - \delta_2\bm{\Theta}[\delta_1\Phi;\Phi]$. It is $\delta$-exact ($\delta\bm{\omega}=0$) and encodes the symplectic structure of field space. The fundamental theorem links it to conserved $(n-2)$-forms when one argument is a gauge transformation. |
| 10 | Noether–Wald Surface Charge ($\mathbf{Q}_\xi$) | Surface Charges & Conserved Quantities | The $(n-2)$-form obtained from the Noether current decomposition $\mathbf{J}_\xi = \mathbf{S}_\xi + d\mathbf{Q}_\xi$, where $\mathbf{J}_\xi = i_\xi\mathbf{L} - \bm{\Theta}[\Lie_\xi\Phi;\Phi]$. Computed via the operator $I_\xi$ as $\mathbf{Q}_\xi = -I_\xi\bm{\Theta}[\delta_\xi\Phi;\Phi]$. For Einstein gravity, it yields the Komar term: $\mathbf{Q}_\xi = \frac{\sqrt{-g}}{8\pi G} \nabla^\mu \xi^\nu (d^{n-2}x)_{\mu\nu}$. Named after Lee, Wald, Iyer, and Wald (1990–1994). |
| 11 | Fundamental Theorem of Covariant Phase Space | Surface Charges & Conserved Quantities | Contracting the presymplectic form with a gauge transformation $\delta_\xi\Phi^i$ yields $\bm{\omega}[\delta_\xi\Phi,\delta\Phi;\Phi] \approx d\mathbf{k}_\xi[\delta\Phi;\Phi]$, where $\Phi^i$ is on-shell and $\delta\Phi^i$ solves the linearized equations. The infinitesimal surface charge $(n-2,1)$-form is $\mathbf{k}_\xi[\delta\Phi;\Phi] = -\delta\mathbf{Q}_\xi + i_\xi\bm{\Theta}[\delta\Phi;\Phi] + d(\cdots)$. Uniqueness is up to a total derivative. |
| 12 | Integrability Condition | Surface Charges & Conserved Quantities | A necessary condition for the existence of a Hamiltonian generator $H_\xi$: $\delta_1 \oint_S \mathbf{k}_\xi[\delta_2\Phi;\Phi] - \delta_2 \oint_S \mathbf{k}_\xi[\delta_1\Phi;\Phi] = 0$ for all $\delta_1\Phi,\delta_2\Phi$. If satisfied, the charge is integrable and defined by path integration $H_\xi[\Phi;\bar{\Phi}] = \int_\gamma \oint_S \mathbf{k}_\xi[\delta\Phi;\Phi] + N_\xi[\bar{\Phi}]$, independent of the path $\gamma$ in field space. |
| 13 | Charge Conservation Criterion | Surface Charges & Conserved Quantities | The surface charge $H_\xi[\Phi;\bar{\Phi}]$ is conserved under continuous deformations of the integration surface $S$ iff $\bm{\omega}[\delta_\xi\Phi,\delta\Phi;\Phi] \approx 0$. For exact (Killing) symmetries, $\delta_\xi g_{\mu\nu} = 0$ so conservation holds everywhere. For asymptotic symmetries, the Killing equation holds only asymptotically ($r \to \infty$), so charges are conserved only at infinity. A third case — symplectic symmetries — also yields bulk conservation despite $\Lie_\xi g_{\mu\nu} \neq 0$. |
| 14 | Charge Representation Theorem | Surface Charges & Conserved Quantities | Under the Poisson bracket $\{H_\chi, H_\xi\} \triangleq \delta_\xi H_\chi$, the conserved charges obey $\{H_\chi, H_\xi\} = H_{[\chi,\xi]} + \mathcal{K}_{\chi,\xi}[\bar{\Phi}]$, where $\mathcal{K}_{\chi,\xi}[\bar{\Phi}]$ is a central extension (2-cocycle) depending only on the reference background $\bar{\Phi}$. The central extension is antisymmetric, satisfies Jacobi identity, and is non-trivial if it cannot be absorbed into a renormalization of $N_{[\chi,\xi]}$. The charge algebra is isomorphic to the diffeomorphism algebra up to this central extension. |
| 15 | Asymptotic Symmetry Group | Surface Charges & Conserved Quantities | The quotient group: $\text{Asymp. Sym. Group} = \frac{\text{Allowed diffeomorphisms}}{\text{Trivial gauge transformations}}$. Allowed diffeomorphisms preserve boundary conditions (their action is tangential to the phase space $G$). Trivial gauge transformations have zero conserved charge. This is the closest analogue in gravity to the global symmetry group of field theories without gravity. |
| 16 | Komar Term | Surface Charges & Conserved Quantities | The Noether–Wald charge evaluated for pure Einstein gravity: $\mathbf{Q}_\xi = \frac{\sqrt{-g}}{8\pi G} \nabla^\mu \xi^\nu (d^{n-2}x)_{\mu\nu}$. Named after Komar's integrals (1959) that give mass and angular momentum of simple spacetimes when evaluated on an asymptotic 2-sphere. For Schwarzschild with $\xi = \partial_t$, one recovers the total mass $M = m/G$. |
| 17 | Iyer–Wald Charge | Surface Charges & Conserved Quantities | The surface charge formula for Einstein gravity obtained from the covariant phase space formalism: $\mathbf{k}_\xi[h;g] = \frac{\sqrt{-g}}{8\pi G} (d^{n-2}x)_{\mu\nu} (\xi^\mu \nabla_\sigma h^{\nu\sigma} - \xi^\mu \nabla^\nu h + \xi_\sigma \nabla^\nu h^{\mu\sigma} + \frac{1}{2}h\nabla^\nu\xi^\mu - h^{\rho\nu}\nabla_\rho\xi^\mu)$. Integrable for exact (Killing) symmetries. The formula has an ambiguity parametrized by $\mathbf{E}[\delta_\xi g, h; g]$, leading to the Abbott–Deser charge when $\alpha=1$. |
| 18 | Abbott–Deser Charge | Surface Charges & Conserved Quantities | An alternative surface charge for Einstein gravity: $\mathbf{k}^{AD}_\xi = \mathbf{k}^{IW}_\xi + \mathbf{E}[\delta_\xi g, h; g]$ where $\mathbf{E} = \frac{1}{16\pi G}(\delta g)^\mu_{\ \sigma} \wedge (\delta g)^{\sigma\nu}(d^{n-2}x)_{\mu\nu}$. This removes the boundary ambiguity differently from the Iyer–Wald prescription. Obtained by Abbott & Deser (1981) and Deser & Tekin (2002) via integration by parts without formal operators. |
| 19 | Barnich–Brandt Charge | Surface Charges & Conserved Quantities | The invariant surface charge defined using the equations of motion: $\mathbf{k}^{BB}_\xi[\delta\Phi;\Phi] = I^{n-1}_{\delta\Phi} \mathbf{S}_\xi[\frac{\delta L}{\delta\Phi};\Phi]$, where $I^{n-1}_{\delta\Phi}$ is Anderson's homotopy operator. Does not depend on boundary terms added to the action. Equivalent to $\mathbf{k}^{AD}_\xi$ for Einstein gravity. Named after Barnich, Brandt (2000) and developed using cohomological methods. |
| 20 | Anderson's Homotopy Operator | Surface Charges & Conserved Quantities | A formal integration-by-parts operator on the variational bicomplex. Constitutive relation: $-d I^n_{\delta\Phi} + \delta\Phi^i \frac{\delta}{\delta\Phi^i} = \delta$ on $n$-forms, and $-d I^p_{\delta\Phi} + I^{p+1}_{\delta\Phi} d = \delta$ on $p$-forms ($p<n$). The presymplectic potential is canonically defined as $\bm{\Theta} = I^n_{\delta\Phi} \mathbf{L}$. $I^{n-1}_{\delta\Phi}$ is used to construct the invariant (Barnich–Brandt) surface charge. |
| 21 | Invariant Presymplectic Current ($\mathbf{W}$) | Surface Charges & Conserved Quantities | The symplectic current defined from the equations of motion: $\mathbf{W}[\delta\Phi,\delta\Phi;\Phi] \triangleq \frac{1}{2} I^n_{\delta\Phi}(\delta\Phi^i \frac{\delta\mathbf{L}}{\delta\Phi^i})$. Differs from the Iyer–Wald presymplectic form by a boundary term: $\bm{\omega} = \mathbf{W} + d\mathbf{E}$ with $\mathbf{E} = -\frac{1}{2} I^{n-1}_{\delta\Phi} I^n_{\delta\Phi} \mathbf{L}$. Called "invariant" because it does not depend on boundary terms added to the action. |
| 22 | 3D Einstein Gravity — No Bulk Degrees of Freedom | 3D Gravity & Holography | In $n=3$, the Riemann tensor has $\frac{n^2}{12}(n^2-1) = 6$ independent components, exactly matching the 6 independent components of the symmetric Ricci tensor. Consequently, the Weyl tensor is identically zero — there is no gravitational wave propagation, no Newtonian potential, and point masses do not attract. Despite this local triviality, the theory admits black holes (BTZ), infinite-dimensional asymptotic symmetries, and a holographic description. On-shell: $R_{\mu\nu} \approx 2\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$. |
| 23 | Weyl Tensor Vanishing in 3D | 3D Gravity & Holography | The Riemann tensor decomposes as $R_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta} = W_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta} + \frac{2}{n-2}(g_{\alpha[\mu}R_{\nu]\beta} + R_{\alpha[\mu}g_{\nu]\beta}) - \frac{2}{(n-1)(n-2)}R g_{\alpha[\mu}g_{\nu]\beta}$. For $n=3$, the Weyl tensor $W_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}$ vanishes identically because the independent components of Riemann (6) saturate those of Ricci (6). This means all curvature is determined locally by the matter distribution; there is no gravitational radiation in 3D. |
| 24 | BTZ Black Hole | 3D Gravity & Holography | The black hole solution of 3D Einstein gravity with negative cosmological constant, discovered by Bañados, Teitelboim, and Zanelli (1992). Given by $ds^2 = -\frac{(r^2-r_+^2)(r^2-r_-^2)}{\ell^2 r^2}dt^2 + \frac{\ell^2 r^2}{(r^2-r_+^2)(r^2-r_-^2)}dr^2 + r^2(d\phi - \frac{r_+ r_-}{\ell r^2}dt)^2$, with mass $M = \frac{r_+^2 + r_-^2}{\ell^2}$ and angular momentum $J = \frac{2r_+ r_-}{\ell}$. Shares features with the 4D Kerr solution. The Bekenstein–Hawking entropy $S = \frac{2\pi r_+}{4G}$ can be holographically computed via Cardy's formula of a dual CFT₂. |
| 25 | Anti-de Sitter Spacetime (AdS₃) | 3D Gravity & Holography | The maximally symmetric vacuum solution of 3D Einstein gravity with $\Lambda < 0$. Global AdS₃ is a solid cylinder with metric $ds^2 = -\cosh^2\rho\, d\tau^2 + \ell^2 d\rho^2 + \ell^2\sinh^2\rho\, d\phi^2$. Its isometry group is $SO(2,2) \simeq SL(2,\mathbb{R})_L \times SL(2,\mathbb{R})_R$. The boundary at $\rho \to \infty$ is a cylinder $\mathbb{R} \times S^1$. AdS₃ is not globally hyperbolic — boundary conditions at spatial infinity must be supplied for well-posed Cauchy evolution. |
| 26 | Brown–Henneaux Boundary Conditions | 3D Gravity & Holography | Dirichlet boundary conditions for asymptotically AdS₃ spacetimes: $g_{(0)ab} = \eta_{ab}$ (fixed boundary metric) in Fefferman–Graham coordinates. The asymptotic symmetry group consists of two copies of the diffeomorphism group of the circle (Diff$(S^1)$), generated by Virasoro algebras with central charge $c_L = c_R = \frac{3\ell}{2G}$. This 1986 result anticipated the AdS/CFT correspondence by 11 years. The boundary conditions lead to finite, integrable, and conserved charges. |
| 27 | Fefferman–Graham Coordinates | 3D Gravity & Holography | A gauge choice for asymptotically AdS₃ spacetimes: $ds^2 = \ell^2 d\rho^2 + e^{2\rho} g_{(0)ab} dx^a dx^b + \mathcal{O}(e^{\rho})$, or equivalently $ds^2 = \ell^2 \frac{dr^2}{r^2} + \frac{r^2}{\ell^2} g_{(0)ab} dx^a dx^b + \mathcal{O}(r)$ with $r = \ell e^\rho$. $\rho$ is a radial coordinate ($\rho \to +\infty$ at the boundary), $x^a = (t,\phi)$ are boundary coordinates. This gauge removes trivial bulk diffeomorphisms; residual diffeomorphisms are parametrized by 3 functions of 2 coordinates. |
| 28 | Conformal Killing Equation on the Boundary | 3D Gravity & Holography | The residual diffeomorphisms preserving Brown–Henneaux boundary conditions are governed by a boundary conformal Killing vector $V^a$: $\mathcal{D}_a V_b + \mathcal{D}_b V_a = \mathcal{D}_c V^c \eta_{ab}$. In lightcone coordinates $x^\pm = t/\ell \pm \phi$, the solutions split into chiral halves: $V^+ = V^+(x^+)$ and $V^- = V^-(x^-)$. This yields two infinite families of asymptotic generators, each forming a Virasoro algebra. |
| 29 | Virasoro Algebra (Asymptotic Symmetry of AdS₃) | 3D Gravity & Holography | The algebra of conserved charges for asymptotically AdS₃ spacetimes: $\{L_m, L_n\} = (m-n)L_{m+n} + \frac{c}{12}m(m^2-1)\delta_{m+n,0}$, with central charge $c = \frac{3\ell}{2G}$. Same for $\bar{L}_m$. The central extension is non-trivial and originates from the 2-cocycle $\mathcal{K}_{\chi,\xi}[\bar{\Phi}]$ in the charge representation theorem. This provides the foundation for the AdS₃/CFT₂ correspondence. |
| 30 | Chern–Simons Formulation of 3D Gravity | 3D Gravity & Holography | 3D Einstein gravity with $\Lambda < 0$ can be rewritten as the difference of two $SL(2,\mathbb{R})$ Chern–Simons theories: $S = S_{CS}[A] - S_{CS}[\bar{A}]$, where $A = \omega^a J_a + \frac{e^a}{\ell} J_a$ and $\bar{A} = \omega^a J_a - \frac{e^a}{\ell} J_a$. Here $e^a$ is the triad, $\omega^a$ the spin connection. The gauge group is $SL(2,\mathbb{R})_L \times SL(2,\mathbb{R})_R$. Surface charges in this formalism are efficiently computed using the Barnich–Brandt method. Discovered by Achúcarro & Townsend (1986) and used by Witten (1988) for quantization. |
| 31 | BMS₃ Group | 3D Gravity & Holography | The asymptotic symmetry group of 3D asymptotically flat spacetimes, obtained as the flat limit ($\ell \to \infty$) of the AdS₃ asymptotic symmetries. Contains supertranslations and superrotations. The algebra is the $\mathfrak{bms}_3$ algebra: $[L_m, L_n] = (m-n)L_{m+n} + \frac{c_L}{12}m(m^2-1)\delta_{m+n,0}$ and $[L_m, M_n] = (m-n)M_{m+n} + \frac{c_M}{12}m(m^2-1)\delta_{m+n,0}$. Central charges $c_L$ and $c_M$ differ from the AdS case. |
| 32 | Flat Limit & Flat Cosmologies | 3D Gravity & Holography | The asymptotically flat phase space of 3D gravity is obtained from AdS₃ via the flat limit $\Lambda \to 0$ ($\ell \to \infty$), scaling coordinates appropriately. Flat cosmologies are time-dependent solutions representing contracting/expanding universes. Spinning particles in 3D flat space are characterized by deficit angles (mass) and time-helical structure (angular momentum). |
| 33 | Penrose Conformal Compactification | Asymptotic Symmetries & Flat Spacetime | A conformal mapping of asymptotically flat spacetime to a finite manifold with boundary, preserving causal structure. Minkowski spacetime is mapped to a finite triangle (the Penrose diagram) via $u = \tan U$, $v = \tan V$ with conformal factor $\Omega = \cos U \cos V$. Boundary points represent: past timelike infinity $i^-$, future timelike infinity $i^+$, spacelike infinity $i^0$, past null infinity $\mathcal{I}^-$, and future null infinity $\mathcal{I}^+$. |
| 34 | Null Infinity ($\mathcal{I}^\pm$) | Asymptotic Symmetries & Flat Spacetime | The 3-surfaces formed by the endpoints of null geodesics: $\mathcal{I}^-$ (past null infinity) is reached as $r \to \infty$ with $u = t-r$ fixed; $\mathcal{I}^+$ (future null infinity) is reached as $r \to \infty$ with $v = t+r$ fixed. Topology is $S^2 \times \mathbb{R}$. Induced metric is degenerate: $ds^2 = 0\cdot du^2 + d\Omega^2$. Radiative gravitational data (Bondi news, gravitational memory) lives on $\mathcal{I}^\pm$. |
| 35 | Spacelike Infinity ($i^0$) and Hyperbolic Foliation | Asymptotic Symmetries & Flat Spacetime | $i^0$ is the singular point in the Penrose diagram where $r \to \infty$ at fixed $t$. It is resolved by a hyperbolic foliation $(\tau,\rho,x^A)$ with $t = \rho\sinh\tau$, $r = \rho\cosh\tau$, where the boundary is now a smooth codimension-1 $dS_3$ hyperboloid. Spatial infinity contains the matching spheres $\mathcal{I}^+_-$ (past limit of future null infinity) and $\mathcal{I}^-_+$ (future limit of past null infinity). |
| 36 | Bondi Gauge | Asymptotic Symmetries & Flat Spacetime | A coordinate system adapted to study gravitational radiation: $ds^2 = -e^{2\beta} \frac{V}{r} du^2 - 2e^{2\beta} du dr + g_{AB}(dx^A - U^A du)(dx^B - U^B du)$, where $u$ is retarded time, $r$ a radial coordinate, and $x^A$ angular coordinates. Fall-off conditions are imposed to define asymptotically flat radiative spacetimes: $g_{AB} = r^2 \gamma_{AB} + \mathcal{O}(r)$, etc. Bondi gauge isolates the physical radiative degrees of freedom. |
| 37 | BMS₄ Group | Asymptotic Symmetries & Flat Spacetime | The asymptotic symmetry group of 4D asymptotically flat spacetimes at null infinity, discovered by Bondi, Metzner, Sachs, and van der Burg (1962). Generated by vector fields $\xi_{T,R}$ parametrized by a supertranslation $T(x^A)$ (arbitrary scalar on $S^2$) and a conformal Killing vector $R^A(x^B)$ on $S^2$. The algebra $\mathfrak{bms}_4$ is the semi-direct sum of supertranslations $\mathcal{S}$ (an infinite-dimensional abelian ideal) and the Lorentz algebra (extended by superrotations). It is larger than the Poincaré group — the Poincaré translations correspond to the first 4 spherical harmonics of $T$. |
| 38 | Supertranslations | Asymptotic Symmetries & Flat Spacetime | The infinite-dimensional abelian ideal of the BMS₄ algebra: $\xi_T = T(x^C)\partial_u - \frac{1}{r}D^A T(x^C)\partial_A + \frac{1}{2}D_A D^A T(x^C)\partial_r + \cdots$, where $T(x^A)$ is an arbitrary function on the 2-sphere. The 4 lowest spherical harmonics ($\ell=0,1$) reproduce Poincaré translations. Supertranslations encode the gravitational memory effect: a burst of gravitational radiation induces a permanent displacement between freely-falling test masses, related to the transition in supertranslation charge. |
| 39 | Superrotations (Extended BMS) | Asymptotic Symmetries & Flat Spacetime | An extension of the BMS group where $R^A$ is any diffeomorphism of the 2-sphere, not necessarily a global conformal Killing vector. Proposed by Barnich & Troessaert (2010–2011). Leads to the extended BMS algebra and is related to the spin memory effect and subleading soft graviton theorem. The corresponding charges are finite and integrable under appropriate boundary conditions. |
| 40 | Gravitational Memory Effect | Asymptotic Symmetries & Flat Spacetime | A permanent change in the gravitational field after the passage of a burst of gravitational radiation. Three types: (1) displacement memory — a net change in the relative separation of test masses (related to supertranslation charge); (2) spin memory — a net time delay between counter-orbiting light rays (related to superrotation charge); (3) center-of-mass memory. Memory effects are intimately connected to asymptotic symmetries and soft graviton theorems in the infrared triangle. |
| 41 | Displacement Memory | Asymptotic Symmetries & Flat Spacetime | The ordinary gravitational memory effect: after a burst of gravitational waves passes between two freely-falling test masses, their relative separation permanently changes by $\Delta \xi^A = -\frac{1}{2} \gamma^{AB} D_B \Delta \sigma$, where $\Delta\sigma = \int du\, N_{AB} N^{AB}$ is the integrated Bondi news squared. The effect is proportional to the radiated energy and can be detected by LIGO/Virgo in the post-merger signal of binary black hole coalescences. |
| 42 | Spin Memory | Asymptotic Symmetries & Flat Spacetime | The subleading gravitational memory effect: a net relative time delay between counter-orbiting light rays on a closed loop. Related to the curl of the Bondi angular momentum aspect. Associated with superrotation charges and the subleading soft graviton theorem. The effect accumulates over many gravitational wave cycles and may be observable with future detectors (e.g., LISA). |
| 43 | Scattering Problem in GR & Junction Conditions | Asymptotic Symmetries & Flat Spacetime | The formulation of gravitational physics as a scattering problem: data on $\mathcal{I}^-$ evolves to data on $\mathcal{I}^+$, with junction conditions at spatial infinity $i^0$ linking the two. Conservation of BMS charges requires matching conditions between $\mathcal{I}^+_-$ and $\mathcal{I}^-_+$ (the corners where null infinity meets spatial infinity). The $S$-matrix in asymptotically flat spacetimes encodes the full infrared dynamics of gravity and connects to celestial holography. |
| 44 | Kerr Metric | Rotating Black Holes | The most general regular, stationary, axisymmetric, asymptotically flat black hole solution of 4D Einstein gravity (Kerr, 1963). In Boyer–Lindquist coordinates: $ds^2 = -\frac{\Delta}{\Sigma}(dt - a\sin^2\theta\, d\phi)^2 + \Sigma(\frac{dr^2}{\Delta} + d\theta^2) + \frac{\sin^2\theta}{\Sigma}((r^2+a^2)d\phi - a dt)^2$, with $\Delta = r^2 - 2GMr + a^2$, $\Sigma = r^2 + a^2\cos^2\theta$. Parametrized by mass $M$ and specific angular momentum $a = J/M$. The Schwarzschild solution is the $a=0$ limit. |
| 45 | Killing Horizon | Rotating Black Holes | A null hypersurface whose null generators are orbits of a Killing vector field. For Kerr, the outer horizon at $r_+ = M + \sqrt{M^2 - a^2}$ is a Killing horizon generated by $\xi = \partial_t + \Omega_H \partial_\phi$, where $\Omega_H = a/(2Mr_+)$ is the angular velocity of the horizon. The surface gravity $\kappa$ is defined by $\nabla_\xi \xi^\mu = \kappa \xi^\mu$ at the horizon. The existence of the horizon bounds $|a| \leq M$ (Kerr bound). For $|a| > M$, the singularity is naked (cosmic censorship violation). |
| 46 | Black Hole Thermodynamics | Rotating Black Holes | The four laws of black hole mechanics (Bardeen–Carter–Hawking 1973), analogous to thermodynamics: (0) surface gravity $\kappa$ is constant on the horizon; (1) $T_H \delta S_{BH} = \delta M - \Omega_H \delta J$ (first law); (2) $\delta A_H \geq 0$ (area increase/second law); (3) $\kappa = 0$ is unattainable (third law). With Hawking radiation (1974), $\kappa$ is identified with temperature $T_H = \kappa/(2\pi)$ and $S_{BH} = A_H/4$ is the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy. For extremal Kerr: $T_H = 0$, $S_{BH}^{ext} = 2\pi M^2 = 2\pi J$. |
| 47 | Ergoregion & Ergosphere | Rotating Black Holes | The region between the event horizon $r_+$ and the ergosphere $r_{erg} = M + \sqrt{M^2 - a^2\cos^2\theta}$ where $\partial_t$ becomes spacelike ($g_{tt} > 0$). Inside the ergoregion, the energy of a particle can be negative as measured from infinity, enabling energy extraction via the Penrose process, superradiant scattering, and the Blandford–Znajek mechanism. The ergosphere touches the horizon at the poles ($\theta = 0,\pi$) and bulges outward at the equator. |
